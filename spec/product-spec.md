# Ember: Product Spec

Nov 1, 2026 · @Michael

## Vision and principles

The app helps Tops and Doms design intentional, consent-driven scenes from what a bottom actually wants, then run them, care for the bottom afterward, and learn from every session. It grows out of the A.C.E. scene design framework and the phone session guides built for real clients. Options, design and customizability are the top priorities.

**Who it serves:** couples who want to use it casually to spice up their relationship, and experienced Tops who want a fully customizable professional toolset. Tops, bottoms and switches all use one app, with a toggle for which side they are on.

**Core scene craft (universal, built into every design):**

- Every scene has an arc: opening, build, peak(s), descent(s), close.
- Warm up before escalating. Start lighter than seems necessary.
- Multiple peaks require active recovery between them.
- Name the landing. The descent is deliberate.
- Aftercare is a deliberate portion of the scene.
- Sub drop risk is always present and always noted.
- Trauma and psychological flags sit where they matter, never buried.
- Consent is continuous. Safe words are confirmed aloud at the start of every scene.

**Design principles:** intuitive, interactive and fun. Sliders over typing. Icons with one word for multiple choice, with an info icon for descriptions. Every element customizable after the fact.

## Users and modes

One app serves both Tops and bottoms from the start. A toggle at the beginning of the interface sets which view a person is in, so switches use one account for both sides and partners communicate directly.

| View | What they do |
| --- | --- |
| Top / Dom | Builds offerings, designs and runs scenes, keeps partner profiles, toys, rituals and programs |
| Bottom | Builds and refines their desire profile, owns their limits, Draw Your Wave, scene requests, SceneVibe previews, debriefs and journal, program homework, post-scene feelings log |

**Roles belong to connections.** People connect by invite, and each connection records who Tops in that dynamic. A switch can Top for one partner and bottom for another. Their private notes as a Top never merge into their own bottom profile.

**The bottom view stands on its own.** It gives bottoms their own reasons to open the app, not just an intake form and a check-in screen. The post-scene feelings log helps catch drop early.

**Guest link fallback.** A Top can send a texted or emailed link for intake and check-ins to anyone not ready to download the app, such as a new client before a first session. They can upgrade to a full account later.

**Simple mode** opens by default in both views: guided steps, automatic designs, a smaller set of choices. **Studio mode** is a toggle away and unlocks custom templates, manual design, programs, custom categories and the full toy directory. Nothing built in Studio is lost if a user switches back.

## Bottom intake

In the bottom view or through a guest link, the bottom starts by choosing how they want to communicate their desires. Every path ends in the same profile, so the Top can design from any of them.

| Path | For whom | What the bottom does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Detailed form | Experienced bottoms, professional sessions | Works through every act and desire, based on the A.C.E. pre-negotiation form, with yes / maybe / no and intensity sliders |
| Three word groups | Newer bottoms, or anyone who finds the full form overwhelming | Picks from three groups of eight words, each with an info icon description |
| Draw Your Wave | Anyone, as a standalone or add-on | Drags a line across the screen to sketch the intensity curve they want along the spectrum of pain and pleasure on the y axis, almost like a number line, with neutral rests in the middle, time on the x axis |

**Three word groups.** The groups are Heat, Hold and Flow, each with eight original options and descriptions (see Word groups below).

**Always collected, whatever the path:** hard limits, soft limits, safe words and hand signal, physical injuries and mobility, triggers and trauma disclosures the bottom chooses to share, overwhelm plan, self-advocacy and safe word history, aftercare needs, emergency contact, preferred honorifics and body language.

**Conflict detector.** When a desire collides with a trigger or injury (for example, restraint wishes against a knee injury and a trigger about being unable to get free), the app flags it for the Top before any design is generated.

**Desire decoder.** When a bottom picks a desire that can mean many things (wanting to be used, controlled or punished, for example), the app asks one follow-up: what does this do for you? Four to six icon answers such as rest from deciding, feeling desired, having a purpose, or losing myself in sensation. The answer shapes the design more than the act itself.

**Skip anything extra.** Every step beyond the safety floor (decoder, maps, word groups, ritual prompts, extra debrief questions) has a visible skip, so streamlined users move straight through. The safety floor never skips.

## The three maps (draft)

Three maps share one gesture: drag a single dot onto a two-axis map. The dot's position, not just its quadrant, feeds the designer, so a dot near the center reads as "a little of everything."

**Desire map** is part of the bottom's intake, alongside or instead of the word groups.

**Aftercare map** can be set twice: once for in-session aftercare and once for the next day. The aftercare section auto-fills with modalities from the dot's area.

**Dynamic map** is placed by both people for each connection. The app checks whether the dots complement each other (a Provide Top with a Receive bottom fits; two Command dots get flagged for a conversation). A.C.E.'s Service Top practice sits in Provide.

All three maps appear in SceneVibe and on the bottom's profile, where past dots show how preferences shift over time.

## Top offerings, custom content and toy directory

Each Top defines what they offer, as broadly or as specifically as they like, and the app only designs scenes from that menu.

**Offerings.** The Top picks the types of play they do and sets a degree slider for each. Detail can go from a category (impact) down to a specific toy.

**Custom everything.** Tops can add custom actions, play categories, aftercare modalities, ritual elements and anything else the defaults miss. Custom items behave exactly like built-in ones.

**Mutual action selection.** After intake, the Top is prompted to choose the actions both people want to engage in, then add notes that shape the scene: surprises, unorthodox combinations of sensations or modalities.

**Toy directory.** A fast way to photograph, describe, inventory, catalog and display every tool and toy.

- Adding a toy takes a photo and a few taps: feel (thuddy / stingy / sharp / other), play type (impact / sensation / insertable / more), and a notes box.
- Optional fields: material and body-safe status, cleaning method, cleaning log, barrier required when shared between bottoms.
- Toys can be added to any scene design, step by step.
- A Top can opt a toy out for a specific bottom. That opt-out is saved to the bottom's profile.

## Scene design

A scene can be generated automatically, built by hand, or filled from a Top's own template, and every version stays fully editable.

**Automatic designs.** The app offers several designs built from the bottom's profile, the Top's offerings and the mutual action list. The Top picks one as a starting point.

**Manual design.** A drag and drop canvas. Beats are placed on a timeline, with sliders for time, intensity and other parameters that shape the scene.

**Editing after design.** Drop-down menu edits on any step. Whole beats and sections can be moved, and steps within beats can be moved. Toys can be added or swapped per step.

**Templates.** A Top can create a few custom templates that capture how they like to work: order, pace, energy. The bottom's profile then fills the template.

**Library.** Scene designs can be saved, reused and modified.

**Rituals.** Opening and closing rituals can be created, named, saved and edited.

- Elements to choose from: words, phrases or names; actions or postures; clothing; objects; music or sound; and custom elements.
- An ideation flow helps the Top brainstorm, name and save the ritual.
- Rituals can be bottom-specific or part of the Top's general ritual list.
- Possible addition: short mid-scene anchors (a phrase or touch) that can be reused across scenes.

## Live Scene Mode and SceneVibe

Every design has two faces: a working guide the Top runs during the scene, and an animated preview that prepares the bottom for the experience.

**Live Scene Mode** is the in-session view, built on the current phone session guide.

- Dark background, readable with one hand in the dark, single taps only.
- Sticky header, safe word pills, limit strip (hard limits red, soft limits amber, physical care blue).
- One card per time block, each with DO, SAY / WATCH and NOTES tabs.
- Color-coded phases with a tappable bottom navigation bar.
- New: running timer per beat, one-tap logging of yellows and moments, a pause button.
- Red button: If red is called, pressing the red button should display desired aftercare, emergency contact and other pertinent information.

**Regulation toolkit.** A one-tap panel in Live Scene Mode for both people, grouped into body (breath, contact, stillness), mind (anchor phrase, focus point), emotion (name it, let it move) and support (check in, slow down, adjust). It surfaces automatically on a yellow and during recovery beats.

**SceneVibe** is an animated, colorful, geometric illustration of the scene's waves, crescendos, pauses and intensity, with simple icons along the way. It communicates the vibe without spelling out every act, so the Top can share it with the bottom ahead of time. It can also play back the bottom's own Draw Your Wave curve beside the Top's design.

**Open question:** how much detail should SceneVibe reveal by default? Surprises the Top planned should stay hidden. Decided: by default the bottom sees only the SceneVibe. The bottom can request the full plan, and the Top chooses whether to send it. The SceneVibe will be general. Perhaps the surprises or special actions have their own behavior in SceneVibe.  The bottom will know the Top has a surprise planned, but won't know what it is.  Impact, sensation, penetration, digital manipulation, should all have geometric and color coded behaviors in SceneVibe.

## After the scene

Every scene ends with reflection on both sides, a timed check-in, and an updated bottom profile.

1. **Top debrief.** Right after the scene, the Top is prompted to add notes and observations, mostly by sliders and taps.
2. **Top self check-in.** A short prompt for the Top's own state, since Tops drop too.
3. **Bottom debrief.** The bottom gets their own reflection prompt in their bottom view or guest link, separate from the Top's.
4. **Check-in reminder.** About 18 hours after the scene, the Top gets a notification to check in with the bottom.
5. **Profile update.** Preferences, boundaries and observations flow into the bottom's profile.

**Bottom profiles** keep a running record of preferences, boundaries, toy opt-outs, rituals and how the bottom evolves over time. The two debriefs appear side by side across sessions.

**Consent snapshots.** The bottom's limits and preferences are locked and dated automatically at the pre-scene check, so the record shows exactly what was in place for that session. The bottom does not approve the plan itself; consent rests on their stated preferences and limits, which the designer cannot break.

**Deeper debrief.** Beyond technique (pressure, pacing, limits), each debrief asks both people one relational question, such as whether they felt seen and mattered during the scene. The bottom also gets one-tap replies: "Loved that, again please," "Yes to this, but another way," "That's a no for me now," and "Something felt off, let's talk."

## Program mode

Program mode builds a multi-session progression toward a goal, with a scene plan, an aftercare regimen and homework for the bottom at each stage.

**Setup questions:** the goal, how many sessions, over how long, and the starting and desired ending points on 1 to 10 sliders.

**Output:** a session-by-session progression, an aftercare regimen matched to each stage, and bottom homework between sessions.

**Non-linear growth.** Progress is a spiral, not a ladder. Plateaus and dips are expected: a session can repeat, step back a level, or pause for integration without counting as failure. The app reshapes the remaining sessions around what actually happened, and the progress view shows the overall trend rather than session-by-session scores.

| Program type | Examples | Guardrails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Endurance and threshold | Pushing pain threshold, longer impact | Minimum recovery days between heavy impact sessions; bruising and skin checks; no step skips a level |
| Control and denial | Edging, orgasm denial for longer periods | Bottom can pause the program at any point; check-ins between sessions |
| Trauma work | Gradually working through a specific trauma | Goal written by the bottom in their own words; app asks about therapist involvement and recommends one; slower default pacing; exit ramp built into every session; app suggests structure but does not script exposure steps |

**Open question:** should trauma programs require a confirmation from the bottom before each session, rather than once at setup? Just once at setup.

## Safety floor, privacy and discretion

A fixed safety floor sits under every design and no user can remove it; everything above it is customizable.

**Safety floor (never removable):**

- Safe word confirmation in the opening of every scene.
- Hard limits shown on screen throughout.
- An aftercare section in every design with customizable time periods.
- Sub drop risk noted, with a check-in reminder.
- A readiness check before the bottom leaves.
- Trauma and trigger flags placed where they matter.

**Profile ownership.** The bottom owns their desires and limits: they can view, edit and revoke the Top's access. The Top owns their private observations, and for switches those notes never merge into their own bottom profile. A Top's note can never override a bottom's stated limit, and limit changes require the bottom's confirmation.

**Privacy.** The app holds health, STI, trauma and mental health information, so storage, encryption and who can see what are core design decisions.

**Discretion.** A discreet mode with a neutral app name and icon, vague notification text, and a PIN or Face ID lock.

## Notifications, messaging and community

Notifications and messaging between connected people ship first; community comes last because it carries the most cost and risk.

**Daily tips.** One notification a day for each role, on by default with an opt-out in Me. Tops get tips and perspectives on topping, aftercare and the psychology of topping. Bottoms get their own library on self-advocacy, knowing and voicing desires, signals, drop and self-care. Respects discreet mode.

**Check-in reminders.** About 18 hours after each scene, plus any program session reminders.

**Direct messaging, phase 1.** Messaging only between people who have connected by invite, with block and report, control over message history, and discreet-mode protections. Bottoms can send scene requests and both sides can share designs within a connection.

**Guest link.** A texted or emailed link for intake, SceneVibe previews, debriefs and check-ins, for anyone not ready to download the app.

**Community, last.** Fantasy scene creation, browsing other Tops and bottoms, sharing designs with others, public or private designs. Requires moderation, age verification, adult content policy and strong protections against outing users.

## Design language

The interface is clean, modern and tactile: people tap, slide and drag far more than they type.

- **Sliders** for intensity, time, degree, experience and any 1 to 10 scale.
- **Icons with one word** for every multiple choice, with an **info icon** for descriptions.
- **Drag and drop** for beats, sections, steps and toys.
- **Drop-down edits** on any element after a design exists.
- **Two visual moods:** a bright, playful look for planning and SceneVibe; a dark, high-contrast look for Live Scene Mode.
- **Phase colors** are customizable by the Top, but default to: amber arrival, purple ritual, green first peak, gray recovery, blue second peak, deep red aftercare.

**Color and texture.** Saturated, textured color: deep jewel tones with grain, print and paper-like surfaces rather than soft pastels and gradients. The look should feel tactile and bold, and distinct from other kink education brands.

**Open question:** does the app keep A.C.E. deep red as its brand accent, or get its own identity so other Tops see it as theirs? Tops should be able to customize the experience to their liking, branding and color choices.

## Build approach (free)

Ember's first working version is built with Claude Code on free infrastructure, with no AI inside the app.

| Piece | Choice | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Builder | Claude Code | Included in a paid Claude plan (Pro or Max); not available on the free Claude plan |
| App type | Installable web app (PWA): opens in the phone's browser and installs to the home screen | Free; no app store fees or adult content review |
| Notifications | Web push for daily tips and check-in reminders, once installed to the home screen | Free |
| Hosting | Free static hosting (for example Cloudflare Pages or Netlify) | Free |
| Accounts, connections, messaging, guest links | Free tier of a hosted database with built-in sign-in (for example Supabase or Firebase) | Free within tier limits |
| Privacy | Sensitive fields (health, triggers, limits, notes) encrypted on the device before upload, so the server cannot read them | Free |
| Scene design | Rules engine from the Design rules tab; no AI calls | Free |

**Why no free AI.** Free AI tiers often allow the provider to use submitted data to train models, and many restrict sexual content. For health, trauma and kink data, both are unacceptable. A model that runs on the phone itself could be explored later.

**Costs that may appear later:** a domain name (about $10 to $15 a year, optional), paid tiers if usage outgrows the free limits, and an Apple developer fee only if Ember ever goes into the App Store.

**Before building:** check the hosting and database providers' acceptable use terms for adult-adjacent apps. Ember stores no explicit imagery, which helps.
